\subsection{Verifiable Capsule}
\label{sec:capsule}

\emph{Capsule is the non-interactive variant of CE authorization}:
the sender encrypts the per-transfer randomness $r_i$ to its
compliance entity at submission time, and proves the encryption is
consistent with the chunk commitments via the same DLEQ that already
links the sender and recipient legs.
No pre-signed \texttt{TransferAuth} struct, no extra round-trip
between sender and \CE\ before broadcast.
A token policy chooses its compliance posture at registration time
via a single flag $\cemode \in \{\None,\, \Capsule,\, \Authorize,\,
\Both\}$, with $\Both$ requiring both a CE signature \emph{and} a
capsule.

\one
\para{Step 1: capsule emission (sender, in parallel with Step 1 of the
authorized flow).}
The sender additionally computes, for each $i \in [0,3]$:
%
\[
  \ctce_i \;:=\; r_i \cdot \ceeksnd,
\]
%
one extra Ristretto point per chunk.
Here $\ceeksnd$ is the encryption key of the sender's \CE\
(\S\ref{sec:notation}), \emph{not} the recipient.
The capsule rides on the same calldata as the chunk triples already
required by~\S\ref{sec:transfer}.

\one
\para{Step 2: DLEQ extension.}
The Step~3 transcript $T_w$ now includes the capsule leg:
%
\[
  T_w := \tagt \cat \cdots \cat \bigl\{\Pchunk{i}, \Rsnd{i}, \Rrcv{i},
        \ctce_i\bigr\}_{i \in [0,3]}.
\]
%
The random-linear-combination aggregate adds one more term,
$\hRce := \sum_{i=0}^{3} \gamma_i \cdot \ctce_i$, and the prover
sends one additional commitment $A_\CE := \alpha_r \cdot \ceeksnd$
along with $A_P, A_\snd, A_\rcv$.
Both the challenge $c$ and the response $s_r$ already cover this
factor; soundness is the same DLEQ proof that already ties the four
legs.
No new assumption.

\one
\para{Step 3: on-chain verification.}
Same procedure as~\S\ref{sec:transfer} with one extra check:
$A'_\CE := s_r \cdot \ceeksnd - c \cdot \hRce$, folded into the
hash that recomputes $c'$.
The chain rejects if $\cemode$ requires a capsule and any $\ctce_i$
is the identity element.

\one
\para{Step 4: CE audit (offline).}
The \CE\ recovers, from chain data alone:
%
\[
  r_i \;=\; \cesksnd^{-1} \cdot \ctce_i,
  \qquad
  v_i \cdot \genG \;=\; \Pchunk{i} - r_i \cdot \genH,
\]
%
then solves a $2^{16}$-bounded DLP for $v_i$ and recovers
$\val = \sum_i 2^{16i} v_i$.
Cost matches the authorized flow's post-hoc recovery
(\S\ref{sec:transfer}, ``CE audit''); the difference is that no
signed struct is needed because the encryption itself is the
authorization artefact.

\one
\para{Modes and floors.}
The token policy fixes a \emph{floor} $\cemode$; each account
binds an \emph{account floor} at \texttt{configureAccount} time
that must be $\succeq$ the token floor in the lattice
$\None \prec \Capsule \prec \Authorize \prec \Both$.
The chain rejects any transfer whose effective mode (the join over
token, sender outbound, and recipient inbound floors) is below
$\Capsule$ when the policy demands $\Capsule$; below
$\Authorize$ when the policy demands $\Authorize$; and that lacks
either ingredient under $\Both$.

\one
\para{Cost summary.}
Capsule adds one Ristretto point per chunk (4 group elements per
transfer), one verifier scalar-mult per RLC leg ($\approx$~$1$ ms),
and zero off-chain round-trips.
It removes one signed-EIP-712 struct per transfer when the policy
floor is $\Capsule$-only.
