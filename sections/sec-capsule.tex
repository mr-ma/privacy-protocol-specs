\subsection{Verifiable Capsule}
\label{sec:capsule}

The verifiable capsule is the non-interactive variant of CE
authorization.
Instead of a pre-signed \texttt{TransferAuth} struct, the sender
encrypts the per-transfer randomness $r_i$ to its \CE\ as part of the
transfer transaction, and proves consistency with the chunk
commitments via the same DLEQ used in~\S\ref{sec:transfer}.

\one
\para{Mode lattice.}
Each token policy fixes a floor
$\cemode \in \{\None \prec \Capsule \prec \Authorize \prec \Both\}$.
Each account binds inbound and outbound floors at
\texttt{configureAccount} time, each $\succeq$ the token floor.
The effective mode for a transfer is the lattice join over the token
floor, the sender's outbound floor, and the recipient's inbound
floor.

\one
\para{Step 1: Capsule emission.}
For each chunk $i \in [0,3]$ the sender additionally computes
$\ctce_i := r_i \cdot \ceeksnd$, where $\ceeksnd$ is the encryption
key of the sender's \CE.
The capsule is included with the chunk triples already required
by~\S\ref{sec:transfer}.

\one
\para{Step 2: DLEQ extension.}
The transcript $T_w$ of~\S\ref{sec:transfer} is extended with
$\{\ctce_i\}$.
The aggregate $\hRce := \sum_{i=0}^{3} \gamma_i \cdot \ctce_i$ joins
$\hRsnd, \hRrcv$ in the random linear combination.
The prover emits one extra commitment
$A_\CE := \alpha_r \cdot \ceeksnd$; no extra response scalar.

\one
\para{Step 3: On-chain verification.}
Identical to~\S\ref{sec:transfer} with the additional check
$A'_\CE := s_r \cdot \ceeksnd - c \cdot \hRce$, folded into the
re-derivation of $c'$.
The chain rejects if the effective mode requires a capsule and any
$\ctce_i = \identity$.

\one
\para{Step 4: CE audit.}
From chain data the \CE\ recovers
$r_i = \cesksnd^{-1} \cdot \ctce_i$ and
$v_i \cdot \genG = \Pchunk{i} - r_i \cdot \genH$, then solves a
$2^{16}$-bounded DLP for $v_i$.
Cost matches the recovery from $\sharedsecret$ in~\S\ref{sec:transfer}.

\one
\para{Cost.}
$+4$ group elements per transfer (one $\ctce_i$ per chunk),
$+1$ scalar-mult per DLEQ verification, no extra round-trips.
$\Authorize$ retains the signed \texttt{TransferAuth};
$\Both$ requires both ingredients.
